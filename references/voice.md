# Voice, Audience, and Literalness

Prose can pass every phrase and structure check and still read as AI. These patterns operate at the level of cadence, audience assumptions, and figuration. Added 2026-07-15 after a proposal rewrite where the phrase-level pass "succeeded" and the result still read as machine writing.

## Machine Cadence

The tell is not any phrase. It is the texture: every sentence a polished assertion, no writer visible, everything symmetrical.

| Pattern | Problem | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Verbless fragment stacks ("High trust, static, the founding layer." / "Anonymous reads, identified writes.") | Reads as slide copy, not a person talking | Give it a verb: "The seed research carries high trust and won't change from here on." |
| Aphoristic closers ("visible momentum recruits the next contribution") | Sentences built to be quoted | Fold into a plain statement of what happens and why |
| Uniform declaratives | Assertion after assertion with no connective tissue | Let sentences reason into each other with "because," "so," "which means" |
| Symmetric scaffolding (bolded parallel blocks, matching triads, every list item the same shape) | Structure doing the work prose should do | Convert some structure to prose; let paragraphs run uneven |
| Every paragraph lands a mini-conclusion | Relentless punchline rhythm | Let some paragraphs end flat, on a plain fact |
| Day-drumbeat vignettes ("Monday. An heir opens...") | One-word scene-setting reads as staged | "On Monday an heir opens..." - narrate like a person recounting |

## Author Presence

An authored piece with no author in it is itself an AI tell. A person writing a proposal, essay, or memo shows up in the prose.

- Use first person where the form allows it: "I keep coming back to that line," "There's one constraint we'd treat as non-negotiable."
- Show judgment and priority: "This is the part we care most about, so we'll spell it out."
- Ask questions a reader would ask, on the page: "Where do they send that? Did it work?"
- Hedge where the author is honestly unsure: "As far as we can tell, each of its limits was a translation cost."

Match person to the piece: a solo essay takes "I," a team proposal takes "we." Confirm which with the author when rewriting on their behalf.

## Insider Asides

Never drop a term, product name, or cultural reference with a knowing nod. The "all the cool kids already know what X means" tone forces readers to pretend. Calibrate to the actual audience of the piece, not to people who share the writer's context.

At first mention, define:

- Terms of art from another field ("skill," "MCP," "read tools," "ontology," "schema," "session exhaust")
- Product and project names the audience may not know (a monitoring service, an internal tool, a startup)
- Cultural or platform references ("Community Notes" needs "on X, where a note only gets shown when people who usually disagree both rate it helpful")
- Shorthand the piece itself invents ("from her seat" needs "meaning from her particular position in the system" once, then use freely)

One clause is usually enough. Define it once, then trust the reader.

## Literal Beats Figurative

Metaphor that gestures at a mechanism hides it. Replace figuration with a statement of what happens.

| Figurative | Literal |
|------------|---------|
| "The graph becomes the source of truth" | "The graph becomes the master version, and reports get generated from it rather than maintained by hand" |
| "Commons fail in known ways" | Name the failures: vandalism, capture by a loud minority, gatekeeping so heavy contributors give up, moderator burnout |
| "The session throws off exhaust" | "The conversation produces information worth keeping" |
| "The tiers shouldn't blend" | "These three trust levels should stay marked on every record" |
| "Waiting at a gate" | "Requiring every contribution to be approved before it appears" |
| "The systems grammar erodes" | "Someone has to keep a feedback loop recorded as a feedback loop and an actor as an actor" |

Test: could a careful reader say, in their own words, what concretely happens? If the sentence only conveys a vibe of the mechanism, rewrite it literally.

## The Override Rule

The rules serve the prose. If applying a rule produces a worse sentence, keep the original.

Real case: "The knowledge exists. It's in the daily practice of the actors the map describes. What's missing is a way for their use of the map to feed the map." The Wh-opener check flags "What's missing is." The rule-compliant rewrite ("the map should be rebuilt so it can take delivery of what they learn") was mud. The original stayed.

Before replacing any sentence, compare the two versions cold. If the replacement is not plainly better - clearer, more concrete, more natural - revert it. A rewrite that trades a working sentence for a compliant one has failed even if every box is checked.
